SMART Planning Advice

Operational reminders, not motivational wallpaper.

Daily

  • Choose one primary outcome for the day.
  • Write actions as observable verbs: read, draft, solve, call, submit, test.
  • Separate fixed commitments from flexible work.
  • Define what “done” means before starting.
  • At day end, carry forward intentionally rather than automatically.

Weekly

  • Prefer 1–3 major weekly outcomes.
  • Use daily actions to support weekly goals; do not make every task a SMART goal.
  • Review blockers and change the plan if evidence says the plan was unrealistic.
  • Send or share the weekly report on a consistent day.
  • Track outputs produced, not only hours spent.

Monthly

  • Pay the credit card statement in full when feasible and by the due date.
  • Review recurring subscriptions and charges.
  • Check upcoming deadlines, travel, renewals, and appointments.
  • Review whether weekly goals are actually advancing semester and yearly outcomes.
  • Archive completed work and clean the active task queue.

Annual

  • Get the vehicle inspected when required and complete annual registration before expiration.
  • Review insurance policies, beneficiaries, emergency contacts, and important records.
  • Review taxes and document-retention needs before filing deadlines.
  • Schedule routine preventive maintenance for vehicle, home, devices, and critical equipment.
  • Review the prior year using evidence: outputs, finances, health routines, relationships, learning, and time allocation.

Goal quality

  • Specific: identify the output or action.
  • Measurable: define an observable completion criterion.
  • Achievable: check capacity and dependencies.
  • Relevant: connect the goal to a larger objective.
  • Time-bound: assign a deadline or review point.

Useful distinctions

  • Goal: desired outcome.
  • Action: behavior taken to reach the goal.
  • Metric: a defined quantitative measure.
  • Indicator: evidence used to judge condition or progress; it may use one or more metrics.
  • Milestone: an intermediate completed state.

Do not overbuild the system

  • If maintaining the planner takes longer than using it, remove fields.
  • Do not track a metric unless it changes a decision.
  • Do not preserve a goal because it was written down; revise it when assumptions change.
  • Do not confuse activity with output.
  • Use the smallest planning horizon that supports the decision.

Recurring checklist ideas

  • Daily: one focus, fixed commitments, next action.
  • Weekly: advisor/friend report, course deadlines, research output.
  • Monthly: bills, subscriptions, budget, calendar scan.
  • Semester: courses, research milestones, administrative requirements.
  • Yearly: registration, taxes, insurance, maintenance, records, major goals.
